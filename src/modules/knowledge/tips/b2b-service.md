# B2B Service Business Tips

## Service Level Agreements, uptime guarantees, and incident response times
Enterprise and business decision-makers require predictable reliability before committing to an external vendor. Check whether the website defines explicit Service Level Agreements (SLAs), such as 99.9% availability, guaranteed response times for critical severity issues (e.g., under 15 minutes), and penalty credits for unmet commitments.

## Data security posture, privacy compliance, and regulatory certifications
B2B buyers cannot compromise their corporate compliance or customer data. Check if the site clearly highlights information security standards, such as SOC 2 Type II attestation, ISO 27001 certification, GDPR, or HIPAA compliance, along with data encryption in transit and at rest.

## Contract engagement models and pricing transparency
B2B procurement teams need to understand how services are billed and structured. Check whether the site explains their engagement models: fixed-price deliverables, monthly recurring retainers, time-and-materials, or dedicated staff augmentation, as well as minimum contract durations or pilot evaluation periods.

## Client onboarding timeline and implementation milestones
Prospective business clients want to know how quickly a solution can be deployed and what internal resources are required from their side. Check whether the website outlines a clear onboarding roadmap, typical rollout duration (e.g. 2 to 4 weeks), technical prerequisites, and kickoff milestones.

## Software integration, legacy compatibility, and API accessibility
B2B organizations already run complex software stacks (CRM, ERP, ticketing, analytics). Check if the website details how the service integrates with standard platforms, whether custom webhooks or REST APIs are provided, and how legacy software constraints are accommodated.

## Case studies with quantified ROI and industry-specific benchmarks
Business stakeholders need peer proof before presenting purchasing decisions to leadership. Check whether the website showcases detailed case studies with measurable business results (e.g., cost reduction percentages, hours saved, error rate drops) rather than vague self-congratulatory praise.

## Dedicated account management and multi-tiered support escalation
Corporate clients need confidence that they will not be routed through generic call centers when urgent issues arise. Check whether the site specifies dedicated account managers, technical success engineers, clear escalation paths, and support coverage hours across global time zones.

## Contract termination, data portability, and vendor lock-in prevention
B2B buyers evaluate offboarding risks before signing agreements. Check if the website reassures prospects regarding contract flexibility, notice periods for cancellation, and guarantees of smooth data export without proprietary lock-in or migration hostage fees.

# Agency and Consulting Business Tips

## Scope boundaries, deliverable definitions, and revision rounds
Clients often fear scope creep, surprise billing, or disagreements over creative direction. Check whether the agency clearly defines what is included in each package or service tier, what counts as an out-of-scope addition, and how many iterative review or revision rounds are bundled into the project scope.

## Delivery methodology, sprint cadences, and project phases
Prospective clients want to understand how an agency structures its creative and technical engagements. Check whether the website clearly walks through the engagement methodology: discovery and market research, prototyping, sprint reviews, testing, and final launch.

## Senior leadership involvement vs junior execution staffing
A common frustration among agency clients is meeting senior partners during sales pitches, only to have actual project execution delegated entirely to junior staff or outsourced contractors. Check if the agency clarifies who actually performs the work and highlights the hands-on involvement of lead strategists and senior creatives.

## Full intellectual property ownership and source file handoffs
Clients commissioning branding, software, design, or custom content need legal certainty about asset ownership upon final payment. Check whether the website explicitly guarantees 100% transfer of intellectual property (IP), copyrights, raw vector files, source code repositories, and production credentials upon project completion.

## Transparent budget baselines, minimum engagement sizes, and rate cards
Vague pricing creates hesitation and attracts unqualified leads. Check if the consulting firm or agency provides baseline guidance: minimum engagement size (e.g. projects starting from $10,000), typical monthly retainer levels, or fixed advisory day rates to qualify serious buyers early.

## Verifiable portfolio case studies with clear client attribution and outcomes
Prospective clients look for proof that an agency has solved challenges similar to their own. Check whether case studies name actual clients, describe the initial problem, outline the strategic intervention, and cite concrete outcomes (conversion lifts, traffic growth, brand reach) rather than anonymous mockups.

## Post-launch maintenance, training, and bug-fix warranty
Project completion is rarely the end of the client journey. Check whether the agency provides a complimentary post-launch warranty period for defects or bugs (e.g. 30 days), staff training sessions for content management, and ongoing retainer options for continuous optimization.
